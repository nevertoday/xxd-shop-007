# XXD Shop 007 · Original brief (English reading translation)

> Reading translation only. The canonical runtime prompt is [zh-CN.md](zh-CN.md).

From the product images, product drawings, exploded views, manuals, structural diagrams or written descriptions supplied by the user, generate an e-commerce product teardown information poster / component breakdown image.

First accurately identify the product type, exterior structure, core components, internal modules, assembly logic, layer relationships, connection methods and main selling points. If the user provides existing drawings, structural notes, part names, teardown order or functional-module descriptions, follow them strictly first; if only an exterior image is provided without clear internal structure, do not invent overly complex internal components out of thin air — only a reasonable, restrained schematic breakdown based on common structures is allowed, staying explanatory rather than pseudo-engineering realism.

If the terms, materials, module names, technical structures or working principles supplied by the user are unclear, first research reliable sources — preferably official brand information, product manuals, professional material or public credible sources — and confirm part names, functional relationships and structural logic before visualising.

The image as a whole uses a clean, restrained, professional, modern product-infographic style. The background stays simple and pure: light grey, light off-white, light warm grey or a low-saturation solid colour, avoiding fancy textures and complex scenes.

The main product sits at the centre in a clear exploded composition, automatically choosing the most reasonable direction for its structure: vertical layered teardown, front-to-back expansion, left-right separation, modules floating apart or a partial cut-away. Spacing between parts is clear and layers distinct, showing the assembly relationship without being too dense or too scattered. Shell, core modules, functional parts, sensors, chips, batteries, filters, lenses, fans, brackets, bases or other key parts should unfold in the real or reasonable structural order.

The product body and parts keep a high-quality 3D product-render feel or a premium product-schematic feel, with clear, real materials, colours and outlines that preserve brand recognition. The overall visual should combine "premium e-commerce infographic + industrial-design showcase", not a rigid engineering drawing and not a cheap popular-science graphic.

Build a clear layout hierarchy:
a small section title at the top, such as "Product Teardown Atlas";
the main title is the product name, large and clear;
a subtitle explains the category and the teardown theme;
around the subject, thin leader lines, numbers, simple labels and short functional notes mark each part;
when necessary, a few information cards help explain local structures, working principles, technical highlights, airflow paths, optical paths, obstacle-avoidance logic, sensor roles or module schematics;
the bottom may carry 3 to 5 core selling-point icons or short phrases summarising the product's advantages.

Text is minimal, clear and professional. Do not pile up manual-style long text. Each label is short and precise, first saying "what this part is" and "what problem it solves". Lines, arrows, numbers and icons share one style, details are orderly, avoiding clutter.

Compositionally, the first glance sees the whole product and the breakdown relationship, the second glance the key modules, the third the labels and selling points. The information hierarchy is clear; not all notes compete at once. Local information cards are supporting and must not overpower the main product.

Colour logic centres on the product's own colours, staying neutral, rational, technological or professional overall. If a functional module needs emphasis, a small accent colour such as blue, green, orange or purple may highlight it locally, but only sparingly, never stealing the show.

The final effect combines product realism, structural clarity, information readability, commercial beauty and e-commerce communication efficiency. Suitable for detail-page headers, core selling-point pages, product technology pages, brand promotion images and premium information posters.

Avoid: arbitrarily invented complex internal structures, wrong part order, lost product recognition, chaotic breakdowns, too many labels, overly long notes, all text the same size, a background that steals the show, overly rigid engineering drawings, cheap infographic styles, excessive decoration, and missing assembly logic.
